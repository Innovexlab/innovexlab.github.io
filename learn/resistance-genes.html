Antibiotic-resistance genes: a clinical guide

What common resistance genes signal, which antibiotic classes they affect, and how to interpret them alongside culture and susceptibility testing.

General medical information for education only, not medical advice. It does not replace advice from a clinician. For the genes each InnovexLab panel reports, see the antibiotic-resistance panel.

Common resistance genes

Gene or familyMechanismAntibiotics affectedClinical note
mecAEncodes penicillin-binding protein 2a (PBP2a), which has low affinity for most beta-lactams.Methicillin/oxacillin and most beta-lactams, in methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) and methicillin-resistant coagulase-negative staphylococci.femA is a Staphylococcus aureus marker often reported with mecA to support an S. aureus source.
vanA, vanBChange the cell-wall building block that vancomycin binds, so it ends in D-lactate instead of D-alanine.Vancomycin (vanA: vancomycin and teicoplanin; vanB: vancomycin, teicoplanin usually active).Characteristic of vancomycin-resistant enterococci (VRE).
CTX-M, SHV, TEMBeta-lactamase gene families: CTX-M (cefotaximase-Munich), SHV (sulfhydryl variable) and TEM (named after the patient in whom it was first found). CTX-M enzymes are the most common extended-spectrum beta-lactamases (ESBLs).Penicillins, cephalosporins including third-generation agents, and aztreonam (ESBL phenotype).SHV and TEM families include narrow-spectrum variants; detection of the family alone does not confirm an ESBL.
AmpCAmpC cephalosporinase (a class C beta-lactamase), chromosomal or plasmid-mediated.Cephamycins (e.g., cefoxitin), third-generation cephalosporins and some beta-lactam/inhibitor combinations.Cefepime and carbapenems are often less affected; inducible chromosomal AmpC matters in some Enterobacterales.
KPC, NDM, VIM, IMP, OXA-48Carbapenemases: KPC (Klebsiella pneumoniae carbapenemase, class A); NDM (New Delhi metallo-beta-lactamase), VIM (Verona integron-encoded metallo-beta-lactamase) and IMP (imipenemase), all class B metallo-beta-lactamases; and OXA-48 (oxacillinase-48, class D).Carbapenems and most other beta-lactams (carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales, CRE).The enzyme class affects which newer agents may retain activity; infection-control notification is usually required.
qnrA, qnrBPlasmid-mediated proteins that protect the bacterial enzymes DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV.Fluoroquinolones (usually low-level resistance).Can combine with chromosomal mutations to produce higher-level resistance.
ermA, ermBEnzymes that chemically modify the ribosome, altering the macrolide binding site.Macrolides, lincosamides (clindamycin) and streptogramin B (MLSB phenotype).Resistance to clindamycin may be inducible.
mefAEfflux pump.Macrolides (M phenotype); clindamycin typically unaffected.Found mainly in streptococci, including Streptococcus pneumoniae and Streptococcus pyogenes.
PVLPanton-Valentine leukocidin, a toxin, not a resistance mechanism.None (virulence factor).Associated with community-associated S. aureus skin infections and necrotizing pneumonia.

Interpreting a resistance-gene result

  • A gene is a genotype. Expression, and therefore phenotypic resistance, can differ.
  • An undetected gene does not prove susceptibility: other mechanisms (porin loss, efflux, target mutations) are not detected.
  • In a specimen with more than one organism, a detected gene cannot be assigned to a specific organism.
  • A gene family may include variants with different spectra; confirm with culture and susceptibility testing where available.
