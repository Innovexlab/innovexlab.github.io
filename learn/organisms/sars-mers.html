Viruses: respiratory

SARS-CoV and MERS-CoV

Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 1 (SARS-CoV-1); Middle East respiratory syndrome coronavirus (MERS-CoV)

General medical information for education only, not medical advice. Treatment considerations summarize published guidance at drug-class level; therapy is chosen by the treating clinician using laboratory results, the clinical picture and current guidelines.

Overview

Coronaviruses that caused the 2003 severe acute respiratory syndrome (SARS) outbreak and, since 2012, Middle East respiratory syndrome (MERS). MERS occurs mainly in the Arabian Peninsula.

Infections

MERS: severe acute respiratory illness and pneumonia, sometimes with kidney failure, linked to the Arabian Peninsula. SARS: severe pneumonia; the virus has not circulated since 2004.

Signs and symptoms

Fever, cough, shortness of breath; consider MERS only with relevant travel or exposure.

Antiviral resistance

No approved antiviral, so drug resistance is not a clinical issue.

Treatment considerations

Supportive care, including oxygen and organ support; no antiviral is approved for MERS. Suspected MERS (fever and respiratory illness after travel to the Arabian Peninsula or contact with a case) needs immediate isolation (see infection control below).

Infection control and public health

A suspected case requires immediate notification of public health and airborne and contact precautions.

  2. American Thoracic Society (ATS) / Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA). Community-Acquired Pneumonia in Adults guideline.
