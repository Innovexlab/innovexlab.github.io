Bacteria: Gram-negative

Enterobacter cloacae complex

General medical information for education only, not medical advice. Treatment considerations summarize published guidance at drug-class level; therapy is chosen by the treating clinician using susceptibility results, local resistance patterns and current guidelines.

Overview

Gram-negative rods common in healthcare settings, notable for inducible AmpC beta-lactamase.

Infections

Urinary tract, respiratory, bloodstream, surgical-site and device-associated infection.

Signs and symptoms

Bloodstream infection, often linked to an intravenous catheter: fever and chills. Urinary infection, often in catheterized patients: fever and flank or suprapubic pain. Pneumonia in ventilated patients: fever, increased secretions and falling oxygen levels. Surgical-site infection: redness, drainage and wound breakdown.

Antibiotic and antimicrobial resistance

Chromosomal inducible AmpC (AmpC); may also acquire ESBL (ESBL genes) or carbapenemase genes (carbapenemases).

Treatment considerations

Guided by susceptibility testing; avoid relying on third-generation cephalosporins for serious infection.
