Understanding your infection test

Your clinician may send a sample to InnovexLab to find the cause of an infection. This page explains, in plain language, what the test does and what happens next.

General medical information for education only, not medical advice. It does not replace advice from a clinician.

What the test does

Infections are caused by germs: bacteria, viruses, fungi or parasites. Every germ carries its own genetic material, like a fingerprint. The test looks for these fingerprints in your sample. It can find a germ without waiting for it to grow in a dish (a culture), the traditional laboratory method.

Some tests also look for antibiotic-resistance genes. These are changes that let some bacteria survive certain antibiotics. Knowing about them can help your clinician avoid an antibiotic that may not work.

Why your clinician might order it

When you have signs of an infection, knowing which germ is responsible helps your clinician choose the right treatment. Your clinician decides whether you need a test, based on your symptoms and examination.

Signs worth telling your clinician about

Urinary tract
Burning or pain when you urinate, needing to go often or urgently, pain low in the belly or in your side or back, or fever. Many older adults have bacteria in their urine without being sick, and that usually needs no test or treatment. Testing helps most when there are symptoms.
Wounds
Redness spreading around a wound, warmth, swelling, more pain, pus or a bad smell, fever, or a wound that is not healing.
Breathing
Cough, fever, sore throat, runny nose or shortness of breath, especially when several people around you are sick at the same time.
Stomach and bowel
Diarrhea that keeps going or contains blood, fever, belly cramps or vomiting.
Nails
Nails that become thick, discolored, brittle or crumbly.
Vaginal health
Unusual discharge, odor, itching or irritation.
Sexually transmitted infections
Discharge, pain when urinating, sores or pelvic pain. Many sexually transmitted infections cause no symptoms at all, which is why screening is recommended for some people even when they feel well.

Get help right away by calling 911 or going to an emergency department if you have trouble breathing, chest pain, a high fever with shaking chills, or if you feel very unwell. This page cannot tell you whether you need care.

Your results

  • Results go to the clinician who ordered the test. Your clinician explains what they mean for you.
  • A positive result means a germ’s genetic material was found. Your clinician decides whether that germ is causing your illness: some germs live on or in the body without causing harm.
  • A negative result means none of the germs the test looks for were found. It does not rule out every possible cause.

Using antibiotics wisely

  • Antibiotics treat bacteria. They do not work against viruses such as colds and flu.
  • Take antibiotics exactly as prescribed, and finish the course unless your clinician tells you otherwise.
  • Don’t share antibiotics or use leftover ones.

Talk to your clinician

If you think this kind of testing could help with an infection you are dealing with, ask your clinician. Your clinician orders the test and receives the results.
