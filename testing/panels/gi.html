InnovexLab Gastrointestinal (GI) panel

Bacterial, viral and parasitic causes of infectious diarrhea identified directly from stool.

General medical information for education only, not medical advice. Targets as listed on InnovexLab’s requisition forms (general requisition form). Detection of a target does not by itself establish infection or determine treatment; results are interpreted by the ordering clinician with the clinical picture and, where indicated, culture and susceptibility testing.

When to consider testing

Acute diarrhea, particularly with fever, blood or mucus, abdominal pain or vomiting, or clusters of cases on a unit. Test unformed stool.

Interpretation

Multiplex detection can identify carriage as well as disease (for example C. difficile); interpret with the clinical picture.

Viruses (9)

  • AdenovirusDNA viruses with many types: some cause respiratory illness and conjunctivitis, enteric types 40 and 41 cause gastroenteritis in children.
  • AstrovirusAn RNA virus causing mild gastroenteritis, mostly in children and older adults.
  • Enterovirus (PAN)A large group of RNA viruses, spread by the fecal-oral and respiratory routes, with a summer and fall peak.
  • Human parechovirusRNA viruses related to enteroviruses; important in young infants.
  • Norovirus GIThe most common cause of acute gastroenteritis and of outbreaks in long-term care, cruise ships and restaurants.
  • Norovirus GIIThe most common cause of acute gastroenteritis and of outbreaks in long-term care, cruise ships and restaurants.
  • Rotavirus AA major cause of severe diarrhea in young children worldwide, much reduced by vaccination; also causes illness in adults and older adults.
  • Rotavirus B/CA major cause of severe diarrhea in young children worldwide, much reduced by vaccination; also causes illness in adults and older adults.
  • SapovirusA calicivirus related to norovirus that causes gastroenteritis and outbreaks.

Parasites (5)

  • Cryptosporidium spp.A chlorine-tolerant protozoan parasite spread through recreational water, animals and person to person.
  • Cyclospora cayetanensisA protozoan parasite associated with imported fresh produce and summer outbreaks.
  • Dientamoeba fragilisAn intestinal protozoan whose role in disease is debated; often found without symptoms.
  • Entamoeba histolyticaA protozoan parasite acquired from contaminated food and water, mainly in travelers and immigrants from endemic areas.
  • Giardia lambliaA protozoan parasite from contaminated water and person-to-person spread, including in child care.
