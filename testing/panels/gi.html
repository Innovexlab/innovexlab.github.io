Gastrointestinal (GI) panel

Bacterial, viral and parasitic causes of infectious diarrhea identified directly from stool.

General medical information for education only, not medical advice. Targets are listed as they appear on InnovexLab’s general requisition form. Detection of a target does not by itself establish infection or determine treatment; results are interpreted by the ordering clinician with the clinical picture and, where indicated, culture and susceptibility testing.

When to consider testing

Acute diarrhea, particularly with fever, blood or mucus, abdominal pain or vomiting, or clusters of cases on a unit. Test unformed stool.

Interpretation

Multiplex detection can identify carriage as well as disease (for example, of C. difficile); interpret with the clinical picture.

Viruses (9)

  • AdenovirusA virus with many types: some cause respiratory illness and conjunctivitis, and enteric types 40 and 41 cause gastroenteritis in children.
  • AstrovirusA ribonucleic acid (RNA) virus causing mild gastroenteritis, mostly in children and older adults.
  • Enterovirus (PAN)One of a large group of RNA viruses spread by the fecal-oral and respiratory routes, with a summer and fall peak.
  • Human parechovirusAn RNA virus related to enteroviruses; important in young infants.
  • Norovirus GIThe most common cause of acute gastroenteritis and of outbreaks in long-term care, cruise ships and restaurants.
  • Norovirus GIIThe most common cause of acute gastroenteritis and of outbreaks in long-term care, cruise ships and restaurants.
  • Rotavirus AA major cause of severe diarrhea in young children worldwide; vaccines against rotavirus A have greatly reduced severe disease.
  • Rotavirus B/CA major cause of severe diarrhea in young children worldwide; vaccines against rotavirus A have greatly reduced severe disease.
  • SapovirusA calicivirus related to norovirus that causes gastroenteritis and outbreaks.

Parasites (5)

  • Cryptosporidium spp.A chlorine-tolerant protozoan parasite spread through recreational water, contact with animals and from person to person.
  • Cyclospora cayetanensisA protozoan parasite associated with imported fresh produce and summer outbreaks.
  • Dientamoeba fragilisAn intestinal protozoan whose role in disease is debated; often found without symptoms.
  • Entamoeba histolyticaA protozoan parasite acquired from contaminated food and water, mainly in travelers and immigrants from endemic areas.
  • Giardia lambliaA protozoan parasite spread through contaminated water and from person to person, including in child care settings.
