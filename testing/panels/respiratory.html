Respiratory panel

Respiratory viruses, bacteria and Pneumocystis identified directly from respiratory swabs, with a Basic Flu option and a reflex resistance panel.

General medical information for education only, not medical advice. Targets are listed as they appear on InnovexLab’s respiratory requisition form. Detection of a target does not by itself establish infection or determine treatment; results are interpreted by the ordering clinician with the clinical picture and, where indicated, culture and susceptibility testing.

When to consider testing

Symptomatic respiratory illness (cough, fever, sore throat, shortness of breath), particularly clusters of cases in a facility where results guide isolation, cohorting and antiviral or antibiotic decisions.

Interpretation

A viral result can support withholding antibiotics. Bacteria detected in upper-airway specimens may reflect colonization.

Bacteria (17)

  • Bordetella pertussisA small Gram-negative coccobacillus; B. pertussis causes whooping cough, and related species cause a similar illness.
  • Bordetella parapertussisA small Gram-negative coccobacillus; B. pertussis causes whooping cough, and related species cause a similar illness.
  • Bordetella holmesiiA small Gram-negative coccobacillus; B. pertussis causes whooping cough, and related species cause a similar illness.
  • Chlamydia (Chlamydophila) pneumoniaeAn obligate intracellular bacterium spread by respiratory droplets.
  • Coxiella burnetiiAn intracellular bacterium from livestock and their birth products; it cannot be seen on routine Gram stain.
  • Haemophilus influenzaeA small Gram-negative coccobacillus of the upper airway.
  • Haemophilus influenzae type bA small Gram-negative coccobacillus of the upper airway.
  • Klebsiella pneumoniaeAn encapsulated gut organism and a major cause of healthcare-associated infection, frequently carrying resistance genes.
  • Legionella pneumophilaA Gram-negative rod from water systems or soil and potting mix, acquired by inhaling contaminated aerosols or dust.
  • Legionella longbeachaeA Gram-negative rod from water systems or soil and potting mix, acquired by inhaling contaminated aerosols or dust.
  • Moraxella catarrhalisA Gram-negative diplococcus of the upper airway.
  • Mycoplasma pneumoniaeA small bacterium without a cell wall that spreads by respiratory droplets, often in households, schools and institutions.
  • Staphylococcus aureusA Gram-positive coccus in clusters carried on the skin and in the nose of many people, and a major cause of skin, soft-tissue and invasive infection.
  • Streptococcus pneumoniaeA Gram-positive lancet-shaped diplococcus colonizing the nasopharynx and the most common bacterial cause of community-acquired pneumonia.
  • Group A StreptococcusA beta-hemolytic Gram-positive coccus in chains; a cause of strep throat, skin and soft-tissue infection and invasive disease.
  • Group B StreptococcusA Gram-positive coccus that colonizes the gut and genital tract.
  • Group C and G StreptococcusA beta-hemolytic streptococcus that causes infections resembling group A Streptococcus, particularly in older adults with other conditions.

  • Pneumocystis jiroveciiA fungus that causes pneumonia in people with weakened immunity, such as people with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) and a low CD4 count (a measure of helper T cells, a type of immune cell), transplant recipients and people on steroids or chemotherapy.

Reflex antibiotic-resistance genes (3)
