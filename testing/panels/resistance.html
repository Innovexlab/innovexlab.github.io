Antibiotic resistance (reflex) panel

Resistance genes reported as a reflex test with the urinary tract infection (UTI), wound and respiratory panels.

General medical information for education only, not medical advice. Targets are listed as they appear on InnovexLab’s general, wound and respiratory requisition forms. Detection of a resistance gene does not by itself determine susceptibility or treatment, and an undetected gene does not prove susceptibility; results are interpreted with culture and susceptibility testing.

When to consider testing

When resistance markers would inform antimicrobial selection, alongside culture and susceptibility testing.

Interpretation

A gene is a genotype; an undetected gene does not prove susceptibility, and in mixed specimens a gene cannot be assigned to one organism.

Targets (11)

Select a gene for its mechanism and clinical significance.

UTI and wound panels (8)

  • AmpCClass C beta-lactamase genes that can inactivate many penicillins and cephalosporins.
  • mecA, femAMethicillin resistance in staphylococci (mecA); femA identifies Staphylococcus aureus.
  • qnrA, qnrBPlasmid-mediated genes that reduce susceptibility to fluoroquinolones.
  • vanA1, vanA2, vanBVancomycin resistance genes, found mainly in enterococci.
  • NDM, KPC, OXA-48, VIM, IMP-7Carbapenemase genes: New Delhi metallo-beta-lactamase, Klebsiella pneumoniae carbapenemase, oxacillinase-48, Verona integron-encoded metallo-beta-lactamase and imipenemase type 7.
  • SHV, TEM, CTX-M1, CTX-M2Extended-spectrum beta-lactamase gene families: sulfhydryl variable, TEM (named after a patient) and cefotaximase-Munich groups 1 and 2.
  • ermA, ermBGenes that cause resistance to macrolides such as azithromycin and erythromycin, and often to clindamycin.
  • mefAAn efflux-pump gene that causes resistance to macrolides but usually not to clindamycin.

Respiratory panel (3)

  • PVL (Panton-Valentine leukocidin)A toxin gene of Staphylococcus aureus linked to severe skin infection and necrotizing pneumonia; not a resistance gene.
  • mecAMethicillin resistance in staphylococci (mecA); femA identifies Staphylococcus aureus.
  • vanA/BVancomycin resistance genes, found mainly in enterococci.
